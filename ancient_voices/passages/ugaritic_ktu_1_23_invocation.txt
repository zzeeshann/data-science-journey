POSITION: KTU 1.23, lines 1-15 (the invocation + the death of Death + the field of El)
TRANSLATION: Karen R. Keen (2010), adapting Mark S. Smith's 2006 translation in *The Rituals and Myths of the Feast of the Goodly Gods of KTU/CAT 1.23* (Society of Biblical Literature)
SOURCE_URL: User-provided PDF of Keen's Duke Divinity School Master of Theology thesis "Beyond Sacred Marriage: A Proposed New Reading of 'Birth of the Beautiful and Gracious Gods' (KTU 1.23)" (April 2010)
LANGUAGE: Ugaritic (translated)
DATE: ~1300 BCE (Late Bronze Age); 2006 CE English translation (Smith), 2010 CE emendations (Keen)
NOTES: Fair-use academic research excerpt. The full Keen thesis adapts Smith's 2006 SBL translation with footnoted emendations. This excerpt is the opening invocation through the death-of-Death pericope — the most thematically threshold-loaded passage in the tablet. The viticultural metaphor (Death as a vine that is pruned, bound, and felled to the terrace; the field as belonging to El and Athirat waRahmay) parallels the death/threshold register the bundle's 12-cluster is supposed to test. **Important methodological caveat for Session 8:** KTU 1.23 is the 23rd tablet in the Dietrich/Loretz/Sanmartín catalogue — its "23" is a museum index, not a content position. KTU 1.23 is therefore *not* a 12-cluster member by the bundle's hypothesis. It is included here as a thematically-threshold Ugaritic wider-sample passage. The chapter for Session 8 should address the catalogue-vs-position methodological question that the Ugaritic case raises about the bundle's framing.

Let me call the Goodly Gods,
Indeed, the beautiful ones, sons of …
Those given offerings on high …
In the outback, on the heights …
to their heads and …
Eat of every food,
And drink of every vintage wine.
Peace, O King! Peace, O Queen!
O enterers and guards!

Death wsr sits
In his hand a staff of childlessness,
In his hand a staff of widowhood.
The pruner prunes him (like) a vine,
The binder binds him (like) a vine,
He is felled to the terrace like a vine.

Seven times it is recited over the dais
And the enterers respond:
"And the field is the field of El
Field of Athirat waRahmay."
