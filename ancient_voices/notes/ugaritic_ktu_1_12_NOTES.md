# Ugaritic KTU 1.12 — working notes (no PD English translation located)

*Status: Tier-1 verification could not be completed for KTU 1.12 within the constraints of this project's open-source-only standard. Ugaritic is therefore **skipped** in Session 8's corpus, with the absence noted plainly in the session record and chapter. The seven-text 12-cluster shrinks to a six-text test (Hammurabi, Enuma Elish, I Ching, Inanna, Pyramid Texts, Gilgamesh — the last two contributing wider-sample passages only).*

## What KTU 1.12 is

A fragmentary Ugaritic mythological text, also catalogued as CTA 12 / RS 2.[012] / UT 75. The clay tablet is partly damaged; only two partial columns survived (length 19.5 cm, width 9.5 cm); the reverse side is blank. First translated by Charles Virolleaud in 1935 (*Syria* 16, "Poème de Ras Shamra," pp. 247–266 — French, in journal still presumably accessible via JSTOR/Persée).

Per Yogev and Yona (2013, "A Trainee and a Skilled Ugaritic Scribe — KTU 1.12 and KTU 1.4," *Ancient Near Eastern Studies* 50: 212–217), the tablet is now believed to be a **scribal exercise** — the work of a trainee, not a master scribe like Ilu–milku (who produced KTU 1.4 of the Baal Cycle proper). The trainee made phonological and aesthetic errors and was likely writing from dictation rather than copying another tablet. This means KTU 1.12 may be textually less reliable than other Ugaritic mythological tablets, which is itself relevant to whether it should sit in any "12-cluster."

Thematically, the text concerns the **exile of disinherited kin** (Schloen 1993, "The Exile of Disinherited Kin in KTU 1.12 and KTU 1.23," *Journal of Near Eastern Studies* 52, no. 3: 209–220) and elements of the wilderness mythology adjacent to the Baal Cycle. It is *not* numbered "12" because it is the twelfth in some thematic ordering — the "1.12" designation is from the Dietrich/Loretz/Sanmartin catalogue of Ugaritic tablets (KTU = *Keilalphabetische Texte aus Ugarit*), which numbers tablets in the museum holdings, not by content position. **This is an important caveat the bundle's "12-cluster" hypothesis didn't anticipate**: KTU 1.12's number is a catalogue index, not a position in the work, so it shouldn't be treated as comparable to "Hammurabi Law 12" or "I Ching Hexagram 12." If the cluster hypothesis is meaningful at all, KTU 1.12 might be the wrong member of it.

## What this means for Session 8

Six of the seven planned texts are now in `passages/`. The cluster falsification test was always a small-N test (seven members); shrinking to six is a real reduction but not a fatal one. Specifically:

- **Position-12 cluster (12-side of the test):** Hammurabi Law 12 ✓, Enuma Elish Tablet 1 line 12 ✓, I Ching Hexagram 12 ✓, Inanna's opening (no controls, but counts on 12-side). Four members.
- **Wider sample (baseline for the permutation):** the position-11 and position-13 controls for Hammurabi, Enuma Elish, I Ching; plus Pyramid Texts Unas Utterances 213/217; plus Gilgamesh Pennsylvania Tablet. Eight members.

The chapter for Session 8 should also note the textual-position complication this paper raised: KTU 1.12's number is a museum catalogue index, not a content-position. If we ever obtain a PD KTU 1.12 translation, it should be added not as a 12-cluster member but as a wider-sample Ugaritic passage — and the cluster hypothesis itself should be re-examined for whether catalogue-number coincidences across texts are even the right thing to test.

## If you ever want to revisit Ugaritic

The cleanest path is probably:
1. **Wyatt 1998 fair-use excerpt.** Same fair-use logic as the Inanna entry: short excerpt (<30 lines), full citation, research purpose. Wyatt is the named gold-standard in the reading guide.
2. **Virolleaud 1935 French original** + a careful human translation by a Ugaritic scholar (not by an LLM, which would fail verification).
3. **A library trip** — most major university libraries have Wyatt 1998, Parker 1997, or de Moor 1987 in physical collections. Photocopying a single ~2-page passage for personal research use is also fair use.

For now, the recommendation is: ship Session 8 with six texts, note the absence honestly, and let Ugaritic remain a Tier-2 follow-up if Session 8's results suggest it would change anything.
