# Summary for a Reader

*~500 words. Self-contained. For someone who has not read the book and wants to understand what this project is.*

---

The starting question was about consciousness — how humans across cultures, eras, and traditions describe the felt experience of being a mind. The original plan listed three sub-questions; the second was chosen at Session 3 and sharpened into a more answerable one: is the worldview shift described in the 2026 book *Thinking in Wholes* — from machine-thinking to systems-thinking — visible in real data on how humans and societies are actually doing? That sharpening turned a metaphysics question into something a beginner data scientist could test.

The hook the country-scale half of the investigation found was not what the question expected. Across 141 countries with complete World Happiness Report data for both 2019 and 2025, the report's six measured factors — income, social support, health, freedom, generosity, perceived corruption — collectively rose in 99 per cent of countries. The unexplained component of the report's regression model fell in 98 per cent. The model expects a substantially happier world than people actually report. Whatever is pulling life evaluations down between 2019 and 2025 sits in the residual. Session 5 ruled out the obvious second guess: 38 of 129 countries showed GDP up, Human Development Index flat, and the happiness decline landing entirely in the residual. Neither income nor the standard development scorecard can see what is hurting people.

The text-level half of the investigation pointed an embedding pipeline at four corpora — *Thinking in Wholes* (2026), an Ackoff lecture on systems thinking, William James's *Principles of Psychology* (1890), and Reddit r/Meditation posts (2010s) — plus fifteen verified primary-source ancient-text passages from Hammurabi, Enuma Elish, the I Ching, Inanna's Descent, and three texts that contributed only as a wider sample after a catalogue-vs-position discovery narrowed the original seven-text plan. The pipeline produced one positive cross-era finding and two clean falsifications. The positive finding: paragraphs of Reddit meditation posts and paragraphs of James 1890 sit semantically adjacent in vector space — top pairs at cosines 0.58 to 0.61, four of five hitting the same paragraph in James about the felt sense of attention turning inward. The falsifications: the structural "position-twelve cluster" across ancient texts turned out to be selection bias (permutation test put it at the 45th percentile of the null), and the "wholeness register reaching across eras" reading of a small cross-era lift turned out to be a formal-prose-register effect once Reddit's conversational English was tested against the same passages.

What the investigation has not found is a name for what is in the country-scale residual. The text-level work, despite the project's chapters initially framing it that way, does not connect statistically to the country-scale work. The two halves use different units of analysis, different data, different outcome variables. The connection between them was rhetorical rather than empirical, and Chapter 10 — the lessons-learned chapter — names that as the project's central methodological lesson.
